correction-2026-09-30-pbo-family-kill: Five strategies were buried by the PBO family kill under verdicts labelled gauntlet-protocol-v6, although v6 states that the family kill is retired. The implementation did not match the chained protocol. This note records what the chain says, what the code did, what was measured when the five were re-judged on their own gates, and what happens to them. It changes no verdict and no state on the chain. The chain is append-only, and the five graveyard entries stand.

WHAT THE CHAIN SAYS. The gauntlet-protocol-v6 note, registry entry 2514 (2026-08-20T22:36:42Z), states: "pbo and pbo_underpowered leave FAIL_ORDER, and the family kill is retired." Its battery is six gates, each a property of the individual strategy alone: sharpe_floor, oos_negative, edge_decay, mc_p05, p_ruin and cost_stress. The same note declares that every individual edge must be standalone when running through the gauntlet, and that a configuration is judged on its own evidence.

WHAT THE CODE DID. Every commit time in this note is UTC, as the chain's times are. The family kill entered gauntlet.py under protocol-v4 as killed_groups (commit 52829359, 2026-08-17T21:21Z), and protocol-v5 rebuilt its trigger as PBO_KILL_PCTILE at 0.95 (commit eeec3f66, 2026-08-20T08:21Z). The v6 build (commit 30e30652, 2026-08-20T23:00Z) took pbo and pbo_underpowered out of FAIL_ORDER and left the family kill untouched. After the six gates had run, a family whose observed PBO sat at or above the 0.95 percentile of its own permutation null was marked killed, and every member of it that had passed all six gates was failed with the reason pbo_family_kill. The kill could not bury a strategy that had failed a gate of its own, so it fired only on strategies that had passed all six. The flag pbo_family_kill was written to every member of a killed family, which is why 91 v6 verdicts carry it at the time of this note; 86 of those 91 failed a gate of their own first (84 on sharpe_floor and 2 on oos_negative) and were buried for that gate. Five were buried for pbo_family_kill.

THE FIVE. Each verdict is a gauntlet verdict labelled gauntlet-protocol-v6 with verdict fail and pbo_family_kill true; each burial is a state_change from gauntlet to graveyard with the reason pbo_family_kill. Times are the chain's ts_utc.

WHAT WAS MEASURED. Each of the five was re-judged by the standalone six-gate battery that the v6.1 worker uses, on price bars truncated at the data_end recorded in its verdict's artifact bundle (config.json, committed by the verdict's artifacts_hash), with the gauntlet's own simulation and the same cutoff. All five PASS. Their chained basis is fail with the burial reason pbo_family_kill, so all five cleared every gate of their own and were buried by the family kill alone. The same comparison was run on a random sample of 60 verdicts that the family kill did not touch (seed 20260930, drawn from the 3,272 such v6 verdicts on the chain at the time of this note) together with the five. All 65 verdicts are identical to the chained ones: for the five, a pass against a base pass buried by the family kill, and for the sample, the same pass or the same failing gate.

THE PARITY RULE, AS ACCEPTED: identical verdicts, and metrics bit-identical when compared under the same engine revision. WHAT WAS COMPARED: the parity tool (tools/gauntlet_parity.py) compares exactly eleven per-strategy gate metrics: is_edge_per_trade, oos_edge_per_trade, edge_decay_pct, mc_p05_equity, p_ruin, cost_stress_net_pnl, train_sharpe, is_edge_raw, oos_edge_raw, is_vol and oos_vol. On those eleven, 54 of the 65 are bit-identical. The other 11 differ in train_sharpe, and in some of them in other metrics computed from the same return series, by at most 17 units in the last place. The largest differences by metric are train_sharpe 17 units in the last place, oos_edge_per_trade and oos_edge_raw 5, edge_decay_pct 3, is_edge_per_trade and is_edge_raw 2, and mc_p05_equity 1. WHAT WAS NOT COMPARED: the tool does not compare the other recorded metrics, including the purged walk-forward report, the era summary, the regime split, the perturbation and, where a verdict carries them, the exit-reason counts (exit_reasons_is, exit_reasons_oos), open_at_end and the benchmark_relative block, so the claim of bit-identity above does not extend to them. A separate recomputation of the 65 covered every per-strategy metric that both the chained verdict and the standalone battery carry, except the perturbation, which it did not recompute. It found a difference in those other metrics only in the 11 above and in one verdict that matched on all eleven gate metrics, a5ceb9a0050a0ad8, whose walk-forward fold 1 net differs by one unit in the last place (0.5228214664922483 chained, 0.5228214664922481 recomputed). WHERE THE DIFFERENCE COMES FROM: all 11 are verdicts dated 2026-08-24 or 2026-08-25. Commit 74b9703b (2026-08-26T23:19Z) changed the engine's arithmetic, replacing statistics.stdev at its two call sites with a float two-pass sample standard deviation, and ENGINE_REV "e2" was introduced with it; the engine before it carried no revision constant. At the time of this note 818 v6 gauntlet verdicts on the chain are dated before that commit. 17 of them are among the 65 compared, and of those 17, 11 differ on the eleven gate metrics and 6 are bit-identical on them. All 48 compared verdicts dated after the commit are bit-identical on the eleven. Run on the same bars with the engine as it stood before 74b9703b, all 11 reproduce every per-strategy metric that both the chained verdict and the standalone battery carry bit for bit, except the perturbation, which that run did not recompute, and the protocol label. One of the five, b09adeb0be6faf4e, is among the 11: its chained train_sharpe is 0.5434340322749764 and the standalone value is 0.5434340322749766, one unit in the last place apart, and its verdict is unchanged. None of the differences changes a verdict.

WHAT HAPPENS TO THE FIVE. Their graveyard entries stand. Nothing is moved out of the graveyard, and nothing re-opens a closed verdict, for the reason v6 gives: re-judging closed verdicts under a later standard is the failure mode this chain exists to prevent. Each of the five is instead re-tried as a NEW strategy id through the existing D9 re-trial path, once the standalone gauntlet worker is live. A re-trial is a new registration, charged to N in full, and it is judged by all six gates from scratch on the data then current. It inherits nothing from the buried entry, neither its verdict nor its passing of the gates. The burial and the re-trial both stay on the chain, so a later reader can see the five were buried for a reason v6 had retired and were then tried again as new strategies.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT CLAIM. It does not claim the five have edge. Passing six standalone gates is what the protocol asks of a strategy before quarantine, and v6 states that a looser gauntlet producing survivors is not evidence of edge. Only the forward record in quarantine can say, and only after it exists. No other strategy was affected by the mismatch: at the time of this note the other 86 family-flagged verdicts failed a gate of their own and were buried for it, and the 3,272 unflagged v6 verdicts (also at the time of this note) were never in a killed family. The statement that verdicts under v6 were standalone was false for these five, and this note is the correction of that statement on the chain.

WHAT CHANGES FROM HERE. The gauntlet-protocol-v6.1 note, chained with this one, records that the family kill is not applied from that note onward and that verify_registry.py rejects it at any later chain line. The five burials before that note remain valid chain history.

WHY THE PBO GATE IS AMENDED, IN ONE PARAGRAPH. Protocol-v4 set a fixed pass line at 0.20 and a family-kill line at 0.50, both presupposing that a family whose siblings have no persistent skill differences scores about 0.5. That presupposition is false in this implementation at small odd family sizes, and is only approximately true anywhere. The CSCV code counts a split as overfit when omega is at most 0.5, where omega is the winner's out-of-sample rank over n_configs plus one. That is Bailey, Borwein and Lopez de Prado's own convention, and at the family sizes their work assumes the boundary case has vanishing measure. At small odd n the median rank lands exactly on the boundary and is swept into the overfit count, making the uniform-rank null n_configs plus one over twice n_configs. At a family of five that is 0.600, which is ABOVE protocol-v4's own family-kill line. Every one of the twelve sibling groups registered before generation 4 was even and carried a null of 0.500. Every one of generation 4's six groups was exactly five. The cause was internal to protocol-v4: its plateau gate requires a registered sibling on both sides of a candidate, making five contiguous values the narrowest useful sweep, so v4's plateau rule drove every family onto the one arm where v4's pbo estimator is biased past v4's own kill threshold.

AMENDMENT 1, THE BOUNDARY TIE COUNTS AS A HALF EVENT. A split whose omega is exactly 0.5 contributes one half rather than one to the overfit count. This makes the uniform-rank null exactly 0.500 at every family size, odd or even, and removes the parity defect at its source. Strictly this amendment is redundant for gating once amendment 3 is in force, because a calibrated null absorbs any fixed bias. It is adopted anyway because the pbo number is RECORDED on every verdict and read by people, and a recorded statistic whose meaning depends on the parity of a family size is a trap for a later reader who does not know to check.

AMENDMENT 2, THE GATE COUNTS DISTINCT CONFIGURATIONS, NOT REGISTERED SIBLINGS, AND FAILS CLOSED BELOW FOUR. The effective configuration count of a family is the number of DISTINCT train-window equity curves among its registered siblings. Siblings whose curves are byte-identical are the same configuration observed more than once: the swept axis did not bind between them, and PBO cannot measure whether selecting among them generalises, because there is nothing to select. Below four distinct configurations the gate does not return a lenient value, it FAILS with reason pbo_underpowered. This follows protocol-v4's own rule for the plateau gate, which fails a family with no swept dense axis rather than passing it vacuously, on the stated ground that a gate which passes on the absence of evidence is not a gate. The same reasoning binds here and in the same direction.

This is not hypothetical. Generation 4's breakout_vol_state_filter registered five siblings sweeping the volatility percentile across its full dense grid, and four of the five produced byte-identical train equity curves: the filter admitted the same seven trades at 0.6, 0.7, 0.8 and 0.9, and only 1.0 differed. Its measured pairwise sibling correlation was 0.999. It scored a pbo of 0.030, the lowest on the chain and the 0th percentile of its own null, and under a naive percentile test it would have been the one generation-4 family to PASS the gate. It should not. Its low score records that a tiny difference between near-identical siblings was persistent, not that a real edge survives selection. Left unhandled, this inverts the gate: the less a swept axis matters, the more robust the family appears.

AMENDMENT 3, THE FIXED THRESHOLDS ARE REPLACED BY A TEST AGAINST THE FAMILY'S OWN NULL. The 0.20 and 0.50 lines are withdrawn. In their place the observed pbo is compared to a null distribution computed for THAT family, by permutation: 200 draws, in each of which the sibling labels are randomly permuted within each day before the pbo is recomputed. Permuting labels within a day preserves that day's cross-section exactly, so the family's real return distribution, its real sibling correlation and the real common market factor all survive, and only PERSISTENT sibling-specific skill is destroyed. That is exactly the null the gate's semantics assume, and computing it per family calibrates the reference to that family's size, parity, correlation and series length rather than assuming values for all four.

The direction of the test is deliberate and is the opposite of v4's. A family PASSES only if its observed pbo falls at or below the 5th percentile of its own null, which is to say only if it DEMONSTRATES that selecting among its siblings generalises better than no skill would. It does not pass by failing to be convicted. A family whose observed pbo falls at or above the 95th percentile is killed entire, as under v4, with every member carrying the pbo_family_kill flag and each keeping its own first failure in FAIL_ORDER as its recorded reason. Anything between fails the configuration and leaves the family alive. Under v4 the burden ran the other way: a family passed unless its score exceeded a fixed line, so a gate with no power passed everything. Under v5 a gate with no power passes nothing, which is the correct direction for a gate standing between a backtest and capital.

THE GATE, STATED AS A PROCEDURE. First, compute the effective configuration count as the number of distinct train-window equity curves; if it is below four, fail pbo_underpowered. Second, compute the observed pbo by CSCV at S equals 16 over all 12870 splits, on the train window only, with every registered sibling in the matrix including screen deaths, and with the boundary tie counted as a half event. Third, compute the family's permutation null over 200 draws by the procedure above. Fourth, if the observed value is at or above the 95th percentile of that null, kill the family. Fifth, if it is at or below the 5th percentile, pass. Sixth, otherwise fail pbo. The train window remains the only window used: the 2024-onward holdout has been consumed three times and this protocol does not consume it a fourth.

WHAT THIS WOULD HAVE DONE TO GENERATION 4, STATED BECAUSE IT IS THE OBVIOUS TEST OF WHETHER THIS PROTOCOL WAS REASONED BACKWARDS. It would have produced ZERO SURVIVORS, exactly as v4 did. The nine strategies that v4 killed on pbo sit at the 62nd and 72nd percentile of their own family nulls, nowhere near the 5th, so they fail amendment 3. The one family that would have cleared the percentile test, breakout_vol_state_filter at the 0th, fails amendment 2 with an effective configuration count of two, and every one of its members had already died at the screen on trade count regardless. The nine sharpe_floor deaths and the one p_ruin death are unaffected, both gates preceding pbo in FAIL_ORDER. This protocol therefore does not resurrect a single generation-4 strategy and was not built to. What it changes is the REASON the generation returned zero, from a threshold that could not have distinguished an overfit family from a clean one at that family size, to a test that can.

COST. A permutation null is 200 additional CSCV evaluations per family. Measured on this machine over a 2327-day train window, one evaluation is about 0.9 seconds at five configurations and about 4.4 seconds at twenty-five, so a null costs about three minutes per five-configuration family and about fifteen minutes per twenty-five-configuration family, or roughly twenty minutes for a six-family generation of the shape generation 4 produced. This is the dominant cost of the gauntlet and it is accepted. It is embarrassingly parallel across draws and across families.

WHAT IS KNOWINGLY NOT FIXED, AND IS THE MOST LIKELY REASON THIS PROTOCOL WILL DISAPPOINT. The pbo estimator is DISPERSE, and amendment 3 calibrates its centre without narrowing it. Measured standard deviation of a single family's estimate is about 0.24 across the six real generation-4 families at five configurations, and in a synthetic sweep that holds everything else fixed it falls only from about 0.21 at five configurations to about 0.19 at twenty-five, so enlarging families buys much less than it appears to. A test demanding the 5th percentile of a distribution that wide is a demanding test, and it is entirely possible that no family this Composer produces will ever clear it. That would mean generation 5 and its successors return zero for a reason no better than the last one, and it is recorded here, before any generation-5 number exists, so that it cannot be discovered later and presented as a surprise. The honest position is that this protocol makes the gate INTERPRETABLE and does not make it POWERFUL, and that no evidence currently on this chain establishes that a real family can clear a calibrated pbo test at all.

RATCHET POSITION. This protocol contains a LOOSENING and is declared as one. Amendment 3 withdraws two fixed thresholds, and families that protocol-v4 would have killed will not necessarily be killed under it, so the permissive direction is real regardless of whether correcting a demonstrable bias would also be defensible as a repair. The standing rule requires the evidence and the argument on this chain before the results a loosening would affect exist. The evidence was chained at entry 2511 and the argument is this note, both before any generation-5 specification has been composed. Amendments 1 and 2 are tightenings and would have been permitted freely on their own. Nothing here is applied to any existing verdict, and the rule that keeps 107 strategies buried is untouched: they stay buried under the protocols that judged them, exactly as generations 1 through 3 did when v4 superseded their standards.

STATED BEFORE ANY RESULT EXISTS. A zero-survivor generation 5 is an acceptable outcome, for the same reason a zero-survivor generation 4 was. The pre-committed consequence carried over from protocol-v4 is carried over again and strengthened: if generation 5 produces no survivors, a per-gate breakdown will again be published on this chain before any threshold is discussed or any successor protocol is drafted, and for the pbo gate that breakdown will additionally report each family's effective configuration count and the full percentile of its observed value against its own null, so that the next reader can tell an underpowered family from a convicted one without rerunning anything.

gauntlet-protocol-v6.1-amendment-1: Amends gauntlet-protocol-v6.1, chained at registry entry 68486, in two things the gauntlet_stats entries record: the expected maximum Sharpe ratio behind the recorded deflated Sharpe is floored, and the PBO permutation null is built by a stated rule for every family that has a verdict needing statistics. It also records that the clustering was made faster without changing any result. Everything else in v6.1, and in v6 as v6.1 left it, stands as written. No gate is added, removed or re-tuned, no threshold moves, and no verdict or state change is touched. Pre-declared before the first gauntlet_stats entry exists: pipeline/gauntlet_stats.py refuses to chain any gauntlet_stats entry until this note is on the chain.

WHAT v6.1 SAYS. Its paragraph HOW trials_n IS RECORDED floors the effective trials count trials_n at the highest cluster count already on the chain, records the clustering's own argmax as trials_n_raw, and says of the cross-cluster Sharpe variance: "trials_sr_var is computed from the current clustering and is not floored". The deflated Sharpe on a gauntlet_stats entry is psr(sr_hat, SR*, T, skew, kurt), where SR* = expected_max_sharpe(trials_n, trials_sr_var) is the expected maximum Sharpe of trials_n zero-skill trials. In pipeline/stats.py, SR* is the square root of trials_sr_var times a factor that depends on trials_n alone, so SR* is proportional to the square root of trials_sr_var.

THE MEASURED PROBLEM. The silhouette quality curve that picks k has two peaks of nearly equal height at very different k: a coarse one below about 100 clusters and a fine one at a few hundred. Which one wins depends on the registry, and trials_sr_var moves with it. On a copy of the registry and the simulation cache, on a random sample of 4,000 registered strategies, the argmax was k = 398 with quality 1.73831 while k = 23 scored 1.73289, 0.31 percent lower. On registration-order prefixes of the same copy the argmax moved between the two peaks as strategies were added, and trials_sr_var moved by about three orders of magnitude: 6.5e-4 at 2,000 strategies (k = 162) and 5.8e-7 at 5,000 strategies (k = 6). On the same copy at data vintage 2026-09-27, over all 13,730 registered strategies, the argmax is the fine peak, k = 560 with quality 1.81564 and trials_sr_var 4.64e-4; the coarse peak is k = 82 with quality 1.70483, 6.1 percent lower, and its clusters give trials_sr_var 1.17e-6, about 395 times smaller. The chain records the same movement. The 1,003 gauntlet-protocol-v6 verdicts that recorded trials_n 302 (registered_n 6,075) recorded trials_sr_var 5.55e-4 and SR* 0.0683; the 676 that recorded trials_n 44 (registered_n 11,860) recorded trials_sr_var 2.04e-6 and SR* 0.00318, about 21 times smaller. trials_n is now floored and trials_sr_var is not, so a night whose argmax lands on the coarse peak would pair the floored N with a coarse-peak variance, and SR* would fall by the square root of the variance ratio: about 30 times for a ratio of 1,000, and about 20 times (0.0665 to 0.00334 at N = 560) for the ratio measured on the full registry. A smaller SR* makes the recorded deflated Sharpe more lenient. Left as it is, the recorded deflated Sharpe could become more lenient as the registry grows, which is what the trials_n floor was introduced to prevent.

THE CHANGE TO SR*. From this note onward the SR* used for the recorded deflated Sharpe on a gauntlet_stats entry is expected_max_sharpe, the larger of expected_max_sharpe_raw and expected_max_sharpe_floor. expected_max_sharpe_raw is expected_max_sharpe(trials_n, trials_sr_var) computed exactly as v6.1 computes it: trials_n floored as v6.1 says, trials_sr_var from the current clustering. expected_max_sharpe_floor is the highest SR* already on this chain: expected_max_sharpe on gauntlet verdicts under protocols v3, v4, v5 and v6, the protocols whose trials_n sets the trials_n floor, and expected_max_sharpe_raw on earlier gauntlet_stats entries. A value that is not a number, is a boolean, is not finite or is not above zero is never a floor. The entry that supplied the floor is named by expected_max_sharpe_floor_entry_hash; on a tie it is the first. Every gauntlet_stats entry records expected_max_sharpe_raw, expected_max_sharpe_floor, expected_max_sharpe_floor_entry_hash and expected_max_sharpe, and keeps trials_n, trials_n_raw, trials_n_floor, trials_n_floor_entry_hash and trials_sr_var exactly as v6.1 records them. trials_sr_var itself is still not floored: the floor acts on SR*, the only quantity through which trials_sr_var reaches the deflated Sharpe. The Harvey-Liu haircut reads trials_n and not SR*, and is unchanged. From this note onward the SR* behind the recorded deflated Sharpe never falls from one gauntlet_stats entry to a later one. At the time of this note, read from a copy of the chain, the highest SR* on the chain is 0.06883413189134865, recorded by 650 gauntlet-protocol-v6 verdicts, the first of them at registry entry 9829 (2026-08-25T23:23:48Z, trials_n 154, registered_n 1,815). The clustering of the 2026-10-03 run, reproduced on a copy at data vintage 2026-09-27 over 13,730 registered strategies, gives trials_n_raw 560 and expected_max_sharpe_raw 0.0665, below that floor.

THE PBO NULL IS BUILT FOR EVERY FAMILY THAT NEEDS STATISTICS. The v6.1 implementation recorded PBO with the gauntlet's helpers and arguments and, as the gauntlet did, built the permutation null only for a family with a passing verdict among the verdicts being statted in that run, so siblings statted on different nights could record different pbo_status for the same family. From this note onward, for every family that holds at least one v6.1 verdict without a gauntlet_stats entry, the permutation null is built whenever the family is measurable, that is, whenever its observed PBO exists and it has at least four distinct configurations, whether or not any of its verdicts passed. A family that is not measurable records pbo_status underpowered, as before. The PBO fields then depend only on the family's members and their train-window series, with the null seeded by the family id as before, so siblings statted on different nights record the same values while the family and its data are unchanged. Each family's result is kept for the current data vintage and data digest and reused, so its null is built once per vintage. The measurement that decided the rule, on a copy of the chain at data vintage 2026-09-27: the 2,193 v6.1 verdicts then without statistics belonged to 286 families, all 286 measurable and 31 of them with a passing verdict. Timed on six of those families, a null cost about 56 times the family's observed PBO computation, which projects to about 32,300 seconds for all 286 families against about 5,000 seconds for the 31. The job has 5.75 hours a night and resumes the next night, so that backlog clears in about two nights.

THE CLUSTERING IS UNCHANGED, ONLY FASTER. The bookkeeping inside the agglomeration in pipeline/cluster.py now keeps each cluster's best merge candidate in arrays and resolves ties with arrays, and the distance arithmetic runs in place. The distance, the average linkage, the tie-break on the distance rounded to 12 decimals and then on the smaller strategy ids, the range of k from 2 to n minus 1, the silhouette quality score and the set of clustered strategies, every buried one included, are untouched. tools/verify_cluster_identity.py proves the change bit-identical. It was run on a copy of the chain with the simulation cache read only, at data vintage 2026-09-27, on random samples of 1,000, 2,000, 4,000 and 8,000 of the 13,730 registered strategies, and for each the full merge history, k, the cluster labels, trials_sr_var and expected_max_sharpe(k, trials_sr_var) were identical to the previous code bit for bit. On the same copy the clustering of all 13,730 strategies (cluster.effective_trials) took 118 seconds and returned k = 560, the argmax the 2026-10-03 run logged; that run's clustering stage had taken 14,927 seconds.

RATCHET POSITION. No gate reads trials_n, SR*, the deflated Sharpe, PBO, plateau_ok or the haircut, under v6.1 or here, so nothing a gate decides changes and nothing loosens. The SR* floor can only raise SR*, so it can only lower the recorded deflated Sharpe: it is stricter, never more lenient. Building the null for more families records more and decides nothing. pipeline/gauntlet_stats.py refuses to chain (exit 1, exit_reason refused_no_amendment_note) until this note is on the chain, detected by the prefix gauntlet-protocol-v6.1-amendment-1: of its first line, first occurrence. The gauntlet worker's refusal check, the rollback gauntlet's family-kill switch and verify_registry.py invariant 12 detect the v6.1 addendum by the prefix gauntlet-protocol-v6.1: with the colon straight after v6.1; this note has a hyphen in that place, so it arms none of them. Runs without --chain are unaffected.

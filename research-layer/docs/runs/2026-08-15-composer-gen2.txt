family two_sided_range_breakout: 6 sibling(s), cites 12 card(s) — The classic range-breakout system buys the n-bar highest high and sells the n-bar lowest low (80a41669681d747b) - a genuinely symmetric rule. Its economic basis here is that the intraday/multi-day range scales with horizon at roughly T^0.59 rather than the random-walk T^0.5 (58d10771de3c6bd0), implying super-diffusive range expansion, and crypto is described as a market with no price anchor where trending effects are observed (6078cc70ec7b8303, 072eb45a179fca95). A volatility-percentile filter is included because label/threshold rules must be scaled to prevailing volatility to remain informative (d0fc8344bd267d47) and because breakouts triggered in the highest-volatility tail are typically the ones already saturated with information arrival (44569b1125588083, f4ecce1a057489c4 motivate treating fixed-time bars as heteroscedastic sampling units). Stop plus time expiry is the triple-barrier exit (295c089b48478986); stops on trend positions add positive skew (e818d7f15744fa91). Sizing via a ~30-day vol estimate (9aeedfe2b4f5e2cc, a2e3769454c39476).
  regime hypothesis: Works when range expansion is super-diffusive and breakouts of either sign continue - a regime of information-driven repricing with directional follow-through. Because entries are direction='both', the family is symmetric in drift: a downside break is taken with the same conviction as an upside break, so a period of zero or negative drift is not automatically fatal. It is explicitly expected to fail in a mean-reverting, low-range regime where n-bar extremes are immediately retraced, and it will also degrade if volatility compresses enough that the ATR stop sits inside normal daily noise. Its P&L depends on the T^0.59 range-scaling persisting, not on the level of drift; if breakout follow-through disappears the family should be retired.
family forced_flow_overshoot_reversion: 8 sibling(s), cites 13 card(s) — Crypto has a documented, mechanical price-insensitive flow: leveraged tokens rebalance at a fixed time each day and must sell more into a fall to hold target leverage (339360601f4277c2), and the larger the rebalance relative to normal volume the larger the impact and the larger the subsequent reversion (01c176a1f1f90ef5). Divergences caused by forced, price-insensitive flow revert, unlike divergences from fundamental repricing (bec5e126dff7ccb1, 89e66b95663d995b). A z-score of price versus its own rolling mean is the standard way to express such an overshoot (1d420cad23545c50 uses exactly this decomposition at the spread level). Two design choices come straight from the cards: only trade the extreme tail of the signal distribution, because the middle of the distribution is mostly noise (4c787022d5d94488, 5a9c8aa95a49e0d3) and fewer, higher-precision bets raise Sharpe under asymmetric payoffs (16278b7e95681e44); and, for a mean-reverting process with a short half-life, Sharpe is maximised by a small profit-take combined with a large stop-loss, with the tight-stop/wide-target combination being the worst possible choice (010bcfbdbae4e5fa, ec52fcd4a87988e9). Hence a wide percentage stop, the smallest available R multiple, and a short time expiry (295c089b48478986). Direction is 'both' so the family harvests upside as well as downside overshoots.
  regime hypothesis: Expects to work in choppy, flow-dominated regimes where leverage unwinds and mechanical rebalancing push price away from a short-horizon anchor and it snaps back within days - the opposite regime to the trend families, and deliberately negatively correlated with them. Its edge is short-horizon reversion of forced flow, so it does not require any drift: a flat-drift, high-churn tape is its best environment. It is expected to lose in exactly the regime the trend families want (sustained directional repricing, where a stretched z-score keeps stretching), and the card evidence is explicit that fundamental-repricing divergences do not converge. If crypto volatility falls to a level where 2-sigma daily excursions are smaller than costs, the per-trade edge vanishes; that is a stated failure mode, but note the signal fires on relative (z-scored) rather than absolute displacement, so it degrades more gracefully than a fixed-threshold rule.
